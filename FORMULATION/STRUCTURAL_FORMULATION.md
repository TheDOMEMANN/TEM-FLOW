# Structural uncertainty formulation

## S1.1 Model and interpretation

Consider a finite tree T with vertex set V, edge set E and a nonempty set L of destination leaves. Leaves not in L are permitted but carry no destination mass. The vector x consists of nonnegative masses indexed by L, with sum M, where M is a known nonnegative constant. No further restriction is imposed on allocation. An unnamed remainder is represented by an ordinary destination leaf. The accounting tree describes nested aggregates, not physical roads or observed journeys.

For each independently acquired record i, choose an edge e and one side of its split. The row Hᵢ sums destination mass on that side. A compatible reading yᵢ satisfies |Hᵢx − yᵢ| ≤ ηᵢ, where ηᵢ ≥ 0 is a fixed absolute error allowance. Bounds form a Cartesian product: no joint error correlation is imposed. Several independent records may measure the same split. Records of the exact total contain no further allocation information. A change of split side is equivalent after replacing yᵢ by M − yᵢ.

A set D specifies records known to be unavailable. The source total remains available. For fixed D, the conditional feasible set contains allocations compatible with the surviving received values. Its coordinate width depends on those values. The prospective width Wℓ(D) is the largest such coordinate width over all compatible possible readings. The budget profile Wℓ,r maximizes Wℓ(D) over sets D of size at most r. This is not the union of feasible sets for unknown corruption identities, and it is not a probabilistic confidence region. Different destinations or budgets may require different maximizing sets and readings.

For two allocations x and x′, a common reading exists for record i exactly when the closed intervals centered at Hᵢx and Hᵢx′, both with radius ηᵢ, intersect. This is equivalent to |Hᵢ(x − x′)| ≤ 2ηᵢ. Their midpoint is a common reading whenever that inequality holds. Consequently, maximizing the absolute destination difference over pairs satisfying the doubled-error constraints gives Wℓ(D). This paired-allocation formulation also applies to crossing groups and supplies the independent general linear calculation.

## S1.2 Attained cut characterization

For an edge e, define cₑ(D) as the smaller of M and every surviving doubled error on that edge. If none survives, use M. For a target leaf ℓ, join every other destination to a common sink with capacity M, and supply ℓ through an incoming edge of capacity M. Regard tree edges as undirected capacity constraints. The maximum transferable amount equals the smaller of M and the minimum capacity of a cut separating ℓ from all other destination leaves.

Upper bound. Let d = x − x′ for any compatible pair, orient the tree arbitrarily and define the transfer on each edge as the signed sum of d on one side. Internal vertices have zero net balance, destination vertices have imbalance d, and the total imbalance is zero. Each transfer is at most cₑ(D) in magnitude, because the corresponding surviving aggregate records bound the side sum and the difference of two masses in [0,M] cannot exceed M. For any cut containing ℓ and no other destination, the sum of signed boundary transfers equals dℓ. Hence |dℓ| is at most the cut capacity and at most M. Taking the smallest cut yields the stated upper bound.

Attainment. Orient a maximum transfer away from ℓ. Since the original network is a tree, cycles may be omitted and the transferred amount w is distributed among other destination leaves as nonnegative amounts aⱼ summing to w. Set xℓ = M and every other coordinate of x to zero. Set x′ℓ = M − w and x′ⱼ = aⱼ at other destinations. Both vectors are nonnegative and have total M. Every group difference is the transfer through its accounting edge and respects each surviving doubled error. The midpoint of the two group totals is therefore a valid shared reading for every surviving record. The target difference is exactly w. This proves the cut characterization and sharpness, including boundary cases with zero capacities.

If M and the truncated capacities are integers, an integral maximum transfer exists, so both attaining allocations can be chosen integer-valued. This assertion concerns the masses; midpoint readings need not be integers. With one destination, conservation forces its mass to M and its width is zero. With M = 0, all widths are zero. The same constructions include unmeasured edges and non-destination dead ends without assigning mass to those dead ends.

## S1.3 Profiles under a shared missing-record budget

Sort the tₑ record errors on each edge in nondecreasing order. After at most j removals on that edge, its largest surviving capacity is aₑ(j) = min(M, 2ηⱼ₊₁) when j < tₑ and aₑ(j) = M otherwise. Removing the j smallest-error records attains that capacity; removing a different set cannot improve it. This reduces each edge to a nondecreasing capacity profile indexed by an integer budget.

For the oriented edge u to v, let Fᵤ→ᵥ(k) be the largest transfer that can pass through that edge to destinations in the component containing v using at most k missing records in that component and on the connecting edge. Let Bᵥ|ᵤ(k) be the corresponding downstream capacity before imposing that edge. If v is a destination, Bᵥ|ᵤ(k) = M. If v has no downstream destination, it is zero. Otherwise, partition the budget among the neighbors w other than u. Maximize the sum of Fᵥ→w(k_w) over nonnegative integer allocations whose sum is at most k, and truncate the result at M. This defines Bᵥ|ᵤ(k). Finally, Fᵤ→ᵥ(k) is the maximum, over 0 ≤ j ≤ min(k,tₑ), of min(aₑ(j), Bᵥ|ᵤ(k−j)).

Correctness follows by induction on the component size. After fixing the removed records on the first edge, transfers through that edge and the downstream component combine in series, so the smaller capacity governs. Downstream branches share no record and combine in parallel, so their capacities add subject to a common budget. Every feasible removal set induces such a budget partition, giving an upper bound. Conversely, independently attaining each branch profile and removing the most precise records on the first edge attains the recurrence. This establishes equality, not only an upper bound.

Each profile can be represented for budgets 0 through r. Combining two branch profiles evaluates all budget splits and costs O((r+1)²). A first traversal computes messages toward a chosen root. A second traversal computes messages in the other direction. Prefix and suffix branch combinations allow each neighbor to be excluded without recomputing every combination. The sum of vertex degrees is 2(n−1); therefore all directed messages require O(n(r+1)²) time and O(n(r+1)) memory. Sorting the m record errors costs at most O(m log(m+1)). Records themselves require O(m) memory. At a destination ℓ with neighbor v, its width profile is Fℓ→ᵥ. The isolated single-destination case has width zero.

The bound applies to a supplied accounting tree and the numerical profiles. Checking and constructing a hierarchy from arbitrary destination sets, solving the observed-data linear programs, and reporting all attaining allocations are separate operations. The public reader documents its interactive size limits. Optional witnesses are reconstructed by tracing the maximizing budget decisions, transferring the resulting attainable mass, and reporting paired allocations and midpoint readings. The tests independently check mass balance, error compatibility and removal counts for these witnesses.

## S1.4 Exact-record threshold and assumption boundary

Assume all errors are zero, M > 0 and there are at least two destination leaves. An edge has capacity zero while any record on it survives and capacity M once every record on it is unavailable. Let qℓ be the smallest sum of edge record counts along a path from ℓ to another destination. For a budget below qℓ, no path to another destination consists entirely of capacity-M edges. The component reachable from ℓ through capacity-M edges therefore has a zero-capacity boundary cut separating it from the other destinations. The prospective width is zero.

At budget qℓ, remove all records on a minimizing path. That path has capacity M, so the entire total can be transferred to its other destination. The prospective width is M and remains M for larger budgets. Thus the profile has the stated zero-to-M threshold. This is a prospective result. Conditional ranges can be intermediate because specific received values restrict the allocation further.

For example, let A+B+U = 100 and observe A+B = 40 exactly. The conditional A interval is [0,40], whereas the prospective A width is 100: another compatible reading could permit all 100 units to move between A and B. The interface reports the two quantities separately. A current impossible reading, such as A+B = 120 under this exact total, is rejected as incompatible even though a prospective design profile can still be calculated.

For a crossing-group example, let four nonnegative destination masses sum to M and observe A+B and A+C exactly. Every difference between allocations compatible with a common pair of readings has the form (t,−t,−t,t). Its positive mass is 2|t|, which cannot exceed M, so each prospective coordinate width is at most M/2. Equality is attained by (M/2,0,0,M/2) and (0,M/2,M/2,0). For M = 100, the width is 50. The two splits cross even after complementation, because all four intersections of their sides contain a destination. They cannot both be tree-edge splits. This example demonstrates why the tree threshold cannot be generalized to arbitrary aggregates.

## S1.5 Relationship to established flow formulations

Flow-based sharp bounds for incomplete tables precede this work [S1]. Budgeted capacity expansion and series-parallel calculations also have established treatments [S2–S4]. The following construction makes the mathematical overlap explicit rather than treating numerical agreement as a failure of the alternative structural formulation.

Fix a target destination and orient its accounting tree toward the remaining destination leaves, which are connected to a common sink. Put an incoming capacity-M edge at the target. Replace every accounting edge with a serial chain containing one stage per associated record. A stage has base capacity min(M,2ηᵢ) and an optional parallel bypass of capacity M at unit cost. Selecting the bypass represents the known removal of that record. For an unmeasured edge use capacity M. Omit non-destination dead ends when forming the formal two-terminal representation.

For any fixed removal set, the source cap truncates relevant flow at M. An unselected stage retains the doubled-error bound; a selected stage no longer restricts any flow up to M. The capacity of the serial chain is thus exactly cₑ(D), after truncation. Branches retain their parallel structure. It follows that the maximum flow of the expanded network equals Wℓ(D), and maximizing over at most r unit-cost bypasses equals Wℓ,r. The expansion has O(n+m) size. For rational capacities, scaling gives an integral-capacity instance of the preceding expansion formulation [S2]. The constructive equivalence itself remains valid for real capacities.

The series and parallel operations in the compact recurrence are therefore inherited from established calculations, including the budget recurrence in Proposition 3.16 of Wolf [S3]. Passing messages for all leaves uses standard tree reuse. The structural contribution presented here is the complete interpretation for independently acquired nested mass records: a sharp uncertainty law with attainment, shared-budget profiles, an exact-record threshold and an explicit boundary of applicability. These derivations and tests do not establish first-in-literature priority, nor superiority over the fastest published specialized algorithm.

## S1.6 Reproduction and software correspondence

The public entry point temflow.run_structural_payload is shared by the command-line calculation, local HTTP service and desktop interface. It verifies an exact nonnegative total, named destinations, record identities, absolute errors and accounting context. Nested input groups are converted to the tree handled by temflow._structural_tree. Crossing groups use the paired-allocation linear formulation and explicit missing-set enumeration. Unsupported extra constraints are rejected instead of silently ignored. The general ERR interface remains available for such constraints.

From the source distribution, install the package and run python -m unittest discover -s tests -v, followed by python -m temflow validate. Run python analysis/structural/validate.py and then python analysis/structural/validate_structural_comparison.py. These scripts import the production tree kernel. All structural inputs are generated synthetically with seed 2026093003. The independent baseline formulates allocation pairs directly in linear programming; it does not call the tree recurrence.

The first script reports 30 continuous cases with 412 target-budget comparisons, 103 enumerated fixed cuts, 24 integer cases with 288 comparisons, 12 exact-record cases with 210 comparisons, 517 checked witnesses, six boundary cases and three invalid-input rejections. The maximum absolute gap against linear programming was 1.1546319456101628 × 10⁻¹⁴. The second script reports 72 cases, 11,229 explicit expansion-versus-cut comparisons and 936 target-budget profile comparisons, with maximum fixed gap 1.7763568394002505 × 10⁻¹⁵. All checks passed in the recorded run. The JSON files preserve each input, comparison and environment description.

The structural example uses 100 kg, destinations A, B and an unallocated remainder, and one exact aggregate reading of 40 kg for A+B. Run python -m temflow structural docs/AGGREGATE_RECORD_EXAMPLE.csv --output evidence-limits.json to reproduce the interface example. The guide explains how to replace these synthetic rows with dated records and how to interpret conditional and prospective outputs.

Run python analysis/python/reproduce_all.py --output analysis/results/REPRODUCTION_REPORT_STRUCTURAL_REVISION.json for the existing empirical analyses. The UK computation is reproduced from public raw records. Dryad, Karg and cross-commodity predictions are rescored against frozen truth. NASS raw microdata are restricted, so only public derived truth is rescored; Zambia values are recalculated from extracted published tables. These different reproduction levels must not be conflated. Timing outputs describe individual local runs, and the structural synthetic checks do not validate African field assumptions.
